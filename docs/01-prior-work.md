# 01 Prior work and novelty verdict (searches run 16 to 17 Oct 2026)

Labels: full text = I opened it; abstract = abstract read through Crossref, OpenAlex or DOAJ; metadata only = title, authors, journal, date and nothing else (closed access, no abstract in Crossref or OpenAlex).

## Searches behind the verdict
- Pack search (`code/search14.py`, log `data/raw/search_log.csv`): OpenAlex (1,701 rows), Crossref (2,128), DOAJ (290), Semantic Scholar (199, partial because of rate limits); 3,585 unique records after merging; every record was read by a rule or by hand, including the 50 whose titles flag a review.
- Targeted synthesis search (`code/priorwork14.py`): nine Crossref `query.bibliographic` strings combining meta-analysis, meta-regression or systematic review with fintech, digital finance, financial inclusion, green finance, digital economy, financial development and carbon emissions, 2015 onward.
- Not searched: RePEc/IDEAS and EconPapers (search needs a form POST, which the working rules forbid), Scopus and Web of Science (no login). The verdict is therefore "not found in the sources I could search", not "does not exist".

## Novelty verdict
- No meta-analysis pooling comparable effect estimates of fintech or digital finance on carbon emissions turned up in any source I could search. The nearest are: a meta-regression of traditional financial development and emissions (2020), a systematic review and meta-analysis on financial inclusion and emissions (2026, not read), and a narrative review of 33 fintech-carbon papers (2026).
- The two items most likely to dilute novelty are the 2026 financial-inclusion meta-analysis and the 2026 narrative review. Sean must read both in full (or an institutional library must) before claiming a first.
- What this pack adds on the data side: 55 studies and 91 estimates extracted with a machine-checked coefficient and SE or t for each, a pre-registered primary estimate, and a venue-quality flag for every study. What it does not add: causal identification, because included papers are observational panels.
